# Structure (reference)

> Article Craft editorial craft guidance (INDUSTRY_BEST_PRACTICE).

## Principles

1. **One article, one promise.** Everything in the article either delivers
   the promise or sets it up. Cut the rest (park it in another article).
2. **Sections answer questions.** A heading is a question the reader has;
   the section is the answer. If you can't phrase a heading as a question
   the reader would ask, reconsider the section.
3. **Order by the reader's need, not the author's discovery order.** You
   learned things in a messy order; the reader should get them in the order
   that builds understanding fastest.
4. **Progressive disclosure.** Simple → specific → advanced. Let readers
   exit early with value gained.
5. **Explicit transitions.** Each section should open by connecting to the
   previous one ("That handles reads — but writes are where this design
   gets interesting").

## Diagnostics the review engine uses

- **Orphan sections**: a section no neighboring section references or
  builds on — candidate for cutting or relocating.
- **Ramp sections**: sections that just restate the previous section at
  greater length.
- **Heading stuffing**: H2/H3 density so high the article is all skeleton
  and no body (also a readability problem — see `readability.md`).
- **Front-loading conclusions**: the "Conclusion" that introduces new
  information. Conclusions consolidate; they don't introduce.
- **Missing payoff**: sections that raise a question and never answer it.
- **Unbalanced depth**: 2,000 words on setup, 200 on the hard part — the
  hard part is the article.

## Outline discipline

A good outline is falsifiable: each section title states the claim or
question, not just the topic. "Kafka" is a topic. "Why Kafka's ordering
guarantee breaks across partitions" is a section.
