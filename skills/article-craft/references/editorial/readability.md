# Readability (reference)

> Article Craft editorial craft guidance (INDUSTRY_BEST_PRACTICE).

## Sentence level

- **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence needs a second read, split it or
  simplify it.
- **Front-load meaning.** Put the subject and verb early; keep modifiers
  close to what they modify.
- **Prefer the active voice** when the actor matters (it usually does in
  technical writing): "The scheduler retries the request" beats "The request
  is retried."
- **Concrete beats abstract**: "adds 40ms at p99 on our benchmark" beats
  "significantly improves performance."
- **Define on first use** — every acronym, every domain term. Even ones you
  think everyone knows.

## Paragraph level

- **3–6 sentences is a good default**; a single-sentence paragraph is a
  tool for emphasis, not a habit.
- **One idea per paragraph**, stated in the first sentence.
- **Rhythm**: vary sentence length deliberately. A short sentence after
  three long ones lands.

## Document level

- **Headings are signposts**, not decoration. If a heading doesn't help a
  skimming reader navigate, cut it.
- **Lists for enumerations, prose for reasoning.** If each bullet needs a
  paragraph of explanation, it's prose, not a list. If your paragraph is
  five parallel items, it's a list.
- **Code blocks**: complete enough to run (or clearly marked as fragments),
  labeled with language, explained after showing.
- **Bold/italics for meaning**, not decoration. If everything is bold,
  nothing is.

## Numbers to watch (heuristics, not laws)

- Sentences over ~35 words: usually split them.
- Paragraphs over ~120 words: usually split them.
- Consecutive one-sentence paragraphs: you're writing bullets without
  bullets.
- More than ~1 heading per 200 words of body text: heading stuffing.
- Adverb density ("very", "really", "extremely", "actually", "simply"):
  usually deletable.

## The read-aloud test

Read the section aloud (or have your agent do it). Where you stumble, the
reader falls. Fix stumbles before polish.
