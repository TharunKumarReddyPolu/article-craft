# Medium Plagiarism Guidelines (reference)

> Source: [Plagiarism Guidelines](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041640213-Plagiarism-Guidelines)
> (official; verified 2026-09-06). See `sources.yaml` → `medium-plagiarism-guidelines`.

Medium requires writers to own the rights or have permission for what they
publish. Violations of copyright and plagiarism rules lead to **suspension
with no warning, appeal, or restoration**; deleting the infringing story is
not grounds for reinstatement.

## What Medium defines as plagiarism (OFFICIAL_REQUIREMENT)

1. Presenting or using someone else's words as your own, without proper
   credit, acknowledgement, or permission.
2. Copying words/text directly from other sources — whole articles,
   paragraphs, or sentences.
3. Slightly rewriting or paraphrasing someone else's work ("mosaic
   plagiarism").
4. Translating or reproducing a source from one language to another without
   authorization; "spinning" a source via translation.
5. **Using AI tools to rephrase, summarize, remix, or otherwise modify
   existing content in a manner that results in a derivative work which
   closely resembles the original content in concept, structure, or essential
   elements.**

Crediting a source does **not** supplant the need to secure permissions.
Proper citations can use numbered superscript formatting or other appropriate
methods.

## What Article Craft does with this (POLICY + HEURISTIC)

The originality workflow (`workflows/originality.md`) treats a supplied source
article as **data for understanding, not a template for rewriting**:

- It extracts the source's central claims, structure, and distinctive
  examples, then compares them with your draft (HEURISTIC): heavy overlap in
  sentence structure, section-by-section structural mirroring, reused
  distinctive examples without attribution, or passage-level similarity are
  flagged as **originality risks** with specific locations.
- It recommends an **independently structured** article: your own
  organization, your own examples, your own experience, with sources cited
  for facts.
- It will not "rewrite this so it doesn't look copied" — that request itself
  describes mosaic plagiarism. The correct response is independent research
  and original composition.
- It never claims to detect plagiarism definitively; it surfaces risk for
  human judgment.
