# Reach: Medium's Official Boost Criteria

How Medium decides which stories get wider distribution — summarized from
Medium's own documentation so the adapter can check your story against the
**official, stated criteria** rather than folklore.

> **What this is not:** a growth hack. Medium states verbatim that its Boost
> elements "should not be interpreted as a checklist of components that
> guarantee Boost" (medium-distribution-guidelines). These checks tell you
> where your story aligns — or collides — with what Medium says it looks for.
> The curation decision remains Medium's.

Sources: `medium-distribution-guidelines` (updated 2026-06-29, verified
2026-09-17), `medium-publish-distribution` (verified 2026-09-17).

## How distribution actually works (medium-publish-distribution)

- **Network Distribution** is the baseline: your followers (and, if
  applicable, publication followers) see the story. Every non-rule-violating
  story gets this.
- **General Distribution** additionally matches the story to readers by
  interest via the recommendations algorithm — homepage feeds, Digest emails,
  topic pages, "More from Medium".
- **Boost** is Medium's highest tier: human curators review stories seven
  days a week and give especially high-quality stories higher priority in
  reader matching. Boosted stories also receive General and Network
  distribution.
- Publishing in a publication adds its followers, Digests, and newsletters to
  your potential audience — an official, mechanical reach advantage.
- "Presentations" in story stats shows how often Medium suggested your story.

## The six official Boost criteria (medium-distribution-guidelines)

Medium evaluates each story "for how it fulfills the overall spirit" of
these elements — not as a pass/fail checklist:

1. **Writer's experience** — "a clear and compelling reason why this
   particular writer is writing about this particular topic"; credible
   first-hand experience; insights integral to the story. Human-created text:
   "does not appear to be generated by Artificial Intelligence, a large
   language model, or other text-generating technology."
2. **Value and impact** — the reader's life is enriched; the story is time
   well spent and worth sharing.
3. **Respect for the reader** — the story is not a sales pitch or a bid for
   attention; it shows "genuine regard for the reader".
4. **Non-derivative** — a fresh perspective; "doesn't just paraphrase,
   recombine, or rehash information that is easily found elsewhere."
5. **Writing and craftsmanship** — well-written, error-free, appropriately
   sourced, narratively strong; length appropriate to purpose; images add
   value with ALT text and credits.
6. **Authenticity and trustworthiness** — pseudonyms are allowed, but the
   story should engender trust.

## What disqualifies a story (official)

- From **Boost**: title/subtitle/cover that don't represent the story well
  (sensationalistic *or* overly generic/mysterious/formulaic), NSFW content,
  non-English stories (curation is English-only today).
- From **General Distribution** (each of these makes the story
  Network-Only): clickbait title/subtitle/cover; unconstructive negativity
  (outrage porn, trolling, rants); low-value content — AI-generated content,
  crypto airdrops, sponsored/PR content, reputation laundering,
  signup/traffic/sales-driven stories, unoriginal summaries, link round-ups,
  incomprehensible writing; misinformation; topic/mentions spamming; Rules
  violations (copyrighted images, dangerous unverified health claims, etc.).
- Stories about Medium or the Partner Program are set to Network
  Distribution only; tag such stories "medium-meta".

## What the reach engine checks (and why)

Every reach check is derived from the above: the six criteria become
advisory readiness signals (first-hand-experience signals, evidence of
reader value, non-derivative signals, craftsmanship mechanics), and each
disqualifier becomes a deterministic pattern check. None of it predicts an
outcome; it surfaces alignment with the official criteria.
