# Medium Formatting (reference)

> Sources: [Writing and publishing your first story](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/225168768-Writing-and-publishing-your-first-story)
> and [Medium Rules](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/213477928-Medium-Rules)
> (official; verified 2026-09-06). See `sources.yaml`.

## What Medium's editor supports (OFFICIAL_REQUIREMENT)

- **Title and subtitle** as dedicated fields at the top of a story (subtitle
  optional but recommended — it is what readers see in feeds).
- Basic formatting: bold, italic, links, quotes, lists, separators, code
  blocks, @mentions.
- **Images**: upload your own, or insert from Unsplash.
- **Third-party embeds** (GitHub, YouTube, etc.): paste the URL on an empty
  line. Embed code is **not** supported; unsupported services become a
  preview card.
- Publish menu: topics (up to 5 tags), story preview customization, paywall
  toggle for Partner Program members.

## Craftsmanship expectations (OFFICIAL_RECOMMENDATION, from Distribution Guidelines)

- Correct formatting is "one of many indicators of good craftsmanship."
- Images should add value (not decoration), with ALT text and credits.
- The story should be readable — well-organized headings, appropriate length
  for its purpose.

## Rules that touch formatting (OFFICIAL_REQUIREMENT, from Medium Rules)

- Embedded content must not collect personal information via Medium forms;
  offsite forms must disclose that the reader is leaving Medium.
- Affiliate links are allowed with FTC-mandated disclosure (a simple sentence
  in the footer works).
- Third-party advertising/sponsorship is not allowed; first-party promotion
  of your own business/newsletter is allowed.
- Don't use tags, links, titles, or other metadata in a misleading way.

## What Article Craft does with this (HEURISTIC)

Markdown-level checks: heading hierarchy (no skipped levels, not
heading-stuffed), paragraph length, code block presence for technical
articles, link quality (descriptive anchors, no bare "click here"), image
lines carry alt text, no HTML that Medium's editor would reject, and embed
URLs on their own lines.
