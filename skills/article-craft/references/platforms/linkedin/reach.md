# Reach: LinkedIn's Official Discoverability Guidance

What LinkedIn officially documents about how articles and posts reach
readers — summarized from LinkedIn's own help material so the adapter
checks alignment with documented guidance, not folklore.

Sources: `linkedin-articles`, `linkedin-article-tips`, `linkedin-posting`,
`linkedin-post-limits`, `linkedin-pcp` (verified 2026-09-07). LinkedIn
publishes no boost program for organic content; documented reach factors
are relevance, professionalism, and the post/article surface itself.

## Official guidance

- **Write from expertise** — "write about specific areas in which you have
  experience and/or expertise" (linkedin-article-tips). This is LinkedIn's
  own first-hand-experience criterion, verbatim.
- **Stay focused** — "keep your writing focused. Avoid covering too many
  topics" (linkedin-article-tips). Breadth dilutes the professional signal.
- **Sufficient substance** — "no limits on word count, but the articles
  that are best received are more than three paragraphs"
  (linkedin-article-tips).
- **Media helps** — official tips recommend media to showcase concrete
  examples (linkedin-article-tips).
- **Right surface** — posts (share box) vs. articles ("Write article") are
  different surfaces with different lifetimes (linkedin-posting); the
  3,000-character post limit is policy (linkedin-post-limits).
- **Professional Community Policies** — the governing baseline
  (linkedin-pcp); policy action is the fastest way to lose reach.

## What the reach engine checks (and why)

Advisory signals only: first-hand-expertise markers, topical focus,
substance floor, and (for adapted posts) the character limit. None of it
predicts feed placement.
